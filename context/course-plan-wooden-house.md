## Course meta

- **Epic tag / folder:** `wooden-house` — files
  `content/locales/en/compass/wooden-house/NN-<slug>.md`, `chapter:` 1–11,
  epic tag first in `tags:`.
- **site.yaml epic (en only):** "The wooden house". **Blurb (the one-line
  promise):** *"Design a wooden house that stays warm, dry and mold-free on a
  cold, wet, snowy site — from reading the land to the stove at its heart,
  natural materials wherever they honestly work."*
- **Audience:** a permaculture designer placing a house inside a whole-site
  design (zones, sectors, scale of permanence, water, aspect) — but every
  chapter must stand for a context-free reader too. Permaculture shapes the
  outline: siting before structure, patterns before details.
- **Stance:** serious building physics, zero eco-romanticism (Daragan
  register). Buhner gives the worldview (wood as a living material, the forest
  as kin, not stock); Ralston disciplines it (measure, log, test claims — the
  reader's own moisture log is the arbiter, and "natural" marketing gets the
  same skepticism as industry marketing); the Health/toxin lens (Asprey)
  backs the mold veto and finish choices. No invented build biography, ever.

### The honesty device (every chapter)

A one-line blockquote sits directly under each Trail block, mirroring
`inside-websites`' engine-room note:

> **Drawing-board course.** We have not built a house yet. This course
> synthesizes established building science and craft tradition onto an
> honestly hypothetical site; every number about the worked terrain is real
> regional data, every scene on it is marked "imagine".

### The worked terrain (the course's recurring site)

An honestly hypothetical homestead site in the **Dinaric karst** (Kočevsko-type
country, Slovenia), introduced in ch. 1 and reused in every worked example
(«imagine a north-facing slope in the Dinaric karst…»):

- ~500 m elevation; 17° mostly north/east-facing slope; relict terraces and
  old village building footprints on the slope's shoulder; dense forest
  (canopy 15–25 m), overstood hazel; brown bears.
- Clay soils over karst — wet ground AND radon country.
- Climate: 8.7 °C mean; 117 frost days; records to −27.6 °C, nearby cold-air
  pools to −32.5 °C — the site itself sits in a **frost pocket**; 1,450–1,500
  mm rain/yr with **no dry season**; snow cover ~63 days, max depth ~1 m,
  heavy snow-load country; traditional roofs there stand at **54°**.

These parameters are real regional data; the site is hypothetical; nothing on
it is lived experience. Chapters teach the general skill first and use the
terrain as the recurring worked case.

## Spine artifact — **the paper house**

A complete, site-specific **house dossier**: a physical folder in which the
reader designs their whole house on paper before the first log is cut —
because nobody builds a house in 11 practices, but everyone can build the
document a real build (or the first honest conversation with a carpenter)
starts from. Plus small physical practices where the domain allows: a wood
billet on a moisture log, a joint carved in scrap, a morning condensation
watch.

| Ch. | Piece added |
|---|---|
| 1 | The folder begun: cover promise ("the smallest house that fits the life" — Mooji sizing) + the **wood log** started (slow arc 1) |
| 2 | **The site sheet** — sun, cold-air, water, access, sector maps of the reader's site (or the worked terrain) |
| 3 | **The approach shortlist** — the comparison table filled for their climate/budget/hands, top two circled |
| 4 | **The ground sheet** — foundation choice + drainage plan + radon note; the **sill watch** started (slow arc 2) |
| 5 | **The joinery card** + a joint carved in scrap wood (physical) |
| 6 | *No new piece — the checkpoint return:* the **committal walk** (site sheet upgraded, decisions committed — see Checkpoint) |
| 7 | **The moisture plan** — one sheet: wall section + air paths + risk rooms; sill-watch harvested into it |
| 8 | **The roof sheet** — pitch, overhangs, covering decision, snow-load note for the engineer |
| 9 | **The hearth sheet** — stove placement at the plan's center, sizing, air supply, clearances, chimney-vs-settling note |
| 10 | **The material schedule + build sequence** — the whole dossier reconciled on the worked terrain; wood log final reading |

| 1 | `01-wood-and-water` | "Wood and water" | Gate | concept | scene | Imagine two village houses: a 150-year сруб (stacked-log house), dark and bone-dry, beside a 20-year modern house with black corners — the course's whole territory is why. Wood follows water (hygroscopic movement, shrinkage across the grain); the mold triangle; **the Gaia bargain** named (natural as much as possible — never at the cost of building physics; mold and stale air void the deal, conventional allowed only past the five-year test); siting before structure previewed; course map; first win = begin the paper house + weigh and measure a billet of local wood (the wood log opens). |

Practice: carve one simple joint in scrap. |
| 6 | `06-the-wool-coat` | "The wool coat" | Path | concept | open question | **Checkpoint.** "What keeps a wall warm — and what keeps it dry? | 7 | `07-the-breathing-house` | "The breathing house" | Path | concept | misconception | "Old houses were healthy because their walls breathed" — autopsy: walls never ventilated a house; leaks dried the wall and froze the family. Sill watch harvested; the moisture plan drawn. |
| 8 | `08-a-roof-for-the-snow` | "A roof for the snow" | Path | protocol | scene | Imagine a meter of snow arriving in one week, then the March slide of it. | 9 | `09-the-hearth` | "The hearth" | Path | concept | artifact-in-trouble | The dossier has walls and a roof — and an empty square in the middle labeled "heat?". ## Checkpoint chapter + the return (frozen)

- **Checkpoint = chapter 6.** Gate quiz right after the Trail block: 5
  retrieval questions spanning ch. 1–5 (wood-and-water, the cold-air river,
  one wall portrait's honest loss, the five-year test, one сруб term), each
  with an answer pointer.
- **The return revisits the site sheet (ch. 2 piece):** the committal walk —
  re-walk (or re-read) the site with four chapters of new capability, upgrade
  the sheet, and commit the three big decisions onto it in ink: footprint
  placement, wall system (from the ch. 3 shortlist), foundation type. Ch. 6
  deliberately adds no new dossier piece; its practice budget belongs to the
  return. The wall-section drawing this chapter teaches lands in ch. 7's
  moisture plan.

## Slow arcs (frozen)

1. **The wood log** — set ch. 1: weigh a split billet of local wood on a
   kitchen scale, measure its width across the grain, write a prediction;
   check ch. 5: re-weigh, re-measure, predict where it stops; harvest ch. 10:
   final reading vs the prediction — the reader has personally watched wood
   follow water, which is the course's ch. 1 thesis measured.
2. **The sill watch** — set ch. 4: each morning log window-sill / cold-glass
   condensation in the reader's current home (room, weather, what ran the
   evening before); harvest ch. 7: read the pattern, see the damp ledger in
   their own data, mark risk rooms and size the airflow on the moisture plan.

## Ideology integration

- **Buhner** leads the worldview: wood as a living, once-alive material; the
  forest as kin — taking trees framed with reciprocity, not as harvesting
  stock (ch. 1, 3); depth of attention as the reader's site-reading tool
  (ch. 2).
- **Ralston** disciplines everything: the wood log and sill watch are
  measure-don't-believe made physical; ch. 11's learn-the-rest backbone is
  his — sources, masters, and claims all tested against the reader's own
  logs. **Symmetric skepticism is a standing rule:** "natural" product
  marketing (paints, membranes, miracle insulations) gets exactly the
  scrutiny conventional-industry claims get.
- **Health / toxin lens (Asprey, Chek)** backs the mold veto (mold is the
  arch-toxin of a tight home) and governs finish and material choices — offgassing,
  formaldehyde-bound panels, fragrance-free oils and soaps (ch. 6, 7).
- **Steiner / Children** touch lightly where the house meets the child: the
  surfaces a child touches and breathes, honest sensory materials (ch. 6).
- **Mooji — «меньше, но лучше»:** the ch. 1 cover promise asks for the
  smallest house that fits the life; recalled when the ch. 10 budget collides
  with square meters.
- **Daragan register:** engage the domain at its most serious — real physics,
  real numbers, named standards; no eco-romanticism, no "cozy cabin" fluff.

## Editorial notes (kept small)

- **Сруб vs frame is a real dispute, not a ranking.** Settling years, log
  craft scarcity and cost against frame speed and insulation depth — ch. 3
  and ch. 10 present both as defensible and show the choice being *reasoned*,
  not revealed.
- **Log mass is not insulation.** Popular сруб romance overstates the thermal
  wall; the course states R-value honestly and lets the stove and envelope
  carry what the logs can't.
- **The masonry stove costs real money and real weight** — its foundation is
  part of its price; the metal-stove alternative is honored as the honest
  budget answer, not mocked.
- **Below grade, natural honestly loses.** Waterproofing, capillary breaks
  and radon barriers are where the five-year test routinely admits
  conventional materials; the course says so plainly instead of green-washing
  around it.
- **The HRV tension stays open.** A heat-recovery ventilator is a machine
  with a filter schedule — and in a tight, cold-climate house it may serve
  the Gaia bargain better than cracked windows serve romance. Ch. 7 presents
  the tradeoff and lets the reader's damp ledger decide.
