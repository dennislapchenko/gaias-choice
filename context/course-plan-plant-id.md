## Course meta

- **Epic tag / route:** `plant-id` — files
  `content/locales/en/compass/plant-id/NN-<slug>.md`.
- **site.yaml epic (en only):** "The named world". **Blurb / one-line
  promise:** *Learn to truly know the plants around you — identify with real
  certainty, keep a naturalist's notebook, and build a personal flora of your
  home ground.*
- **5-chapter mapping:** Gate = 1, Path = 2–4, Summit = 5. Checkpoint-lite
  (not a whole chapter) rides inside ch. 4 — see below.
- **The commission (owner, verbatim):** "how can i arm myself with
  identification, note taking and 'collecting' about plants" — three
  capabilities woven, never three silos: knowing what stands in front of you,
  recording the meeting, keeping a personal record.
- **Audience:** the herbalism reader — a curious adult, often with a child
  along. A child is the best identification companion: they force you to slow
  down and answer "what's that?". **Practices happen outdoors** — this is the
  most naturally away-from-screen course on the site.
- **Stance:** Buhner leads (a plant is a living being you meet — and naming
  is the respect precondition: you cannot be in relationship with a being you
  can't tell from its deadly twin). Ralston disciplines (certainty levels ARE
  not-knowing made practical; claim only the level you verified). Daragan
  register: real botany — characters, families, binomials — never
  app-roulette dressed as nature connection. No medical/edibility claims;
  identification is this course's whole scope, use belongs to herbalism's
  safety frame.

## Spine artifact — the personal flora

A **personal flora of the reader's home ground** (the ~1 km they can walk
daily), living in three physical parts: a bound **field notebook**, a
**starter herbarium of 5 mounted sheets**, and a **life-list** (one line per
plant met, with its certainty level). Ch. 1 starts it: plant #1 genuinely
identified to a stated certainty level, notebook opened, life-list line #1.
Every chapter adds a named piece (table below). Ch. 5 completes it — sheets
mounted, list organized, the flora judged against ch. 1's promise.

The core builds ONE decision instrument — **the verdict workflow**: family gestalt first → characters against a real key/field guide → app as one more hypothesis (its confidence score gets the same skepticism as internet folklore — symmetric skepticism) → **the look-alike check** (mandatory: "what else could this be, and how do I exclude it?") → three independent sources close it (**the three-source rule**, born in herbalism ch. 8 — this chapter is its full training). ## Checkpoint-lite (5-chapter rule: a beat inside ch. 3 or 4, not a chapter)

- **Chapter 4.** Right after the Trail block: a **3-question gate quiz**
  spanning ch. 1–3, each question with an answer pointer — (1) the certainty
  ladder's levels and what promotes a plant one rung *(ch. 1 → "The certainty
  ladder")*; (2) the three giveaway characters of the umbel family and why
  that family demands the look-alike check *(ch. 2 → the umbel portrait)*;
  (3) the verdict workflow's order and where an app is allowed to sit in it
  *(ch. 3 → "The verdict workflow")*.
- **The return:** ch. 4's practice re-identifies **plant #1** (the ch.-1
  piece) with the full toolkit and upgrades its notebook line to a complete
  first-meeting record — re-encoding at current skill, alongside the
  chapter's new piece (the pressing stack).

## Slow arcs (set → harvest, frozen)

- **Arc A — the pressing:** set in **ch. 2** (one leaf/flower of a
  confidently-known common plant into a book press, physically addressed to
  ch. 5), reinforced in ch. 4 (proper technique, 3–4 more sheets), harvested
  in **ch. 5** (mounting + labeling — the ch. 2 pressing is ready by any
  reading pace; ch. 4's sheets mount as they dry).
- **Arc B — same plant, revisited:** plant #1 met in **ch. 1**, re-identified
  and its record upgraded in **ch. 4** (the return), its seasonal change
  logged in **ch. 5**. The reader watches one plant long enough to see it
  move.

## Ideology integration

- **Buhner leads:** plants as living beings you meet, not data you log — and
  identification is the RESPECT precondition: relationship begins where
  guessing ends. Ch. 2's sensing (all senses but taste) is his direct
  perception, framed as "test it yourself".
- **Ralston disciplines:** the certainty ladder is not-knowing made
  practical — the notebook records the level you *earned*, never the one you
  want; the Summit teaches source evaluation (keys, local floras, societies)
  over received folklore.
- **Steiner / Swan** flavor the child-companion thread: the child's "what's
  that?" opens ch. 1; practices note the child as the best slowing-down
  companion; wonder is a developmental need, and a parent who can answer
  with a real name feeds it.
- **Mooji:** ten plants known deeply beat a thousand logged — ch. 5's beat;
  the life-list measures relationship, not score.
- **Daragan register:** real botany — characters, families, keys, binomials —
  engaged at its most serious or not at all. No gamified logging-app framing.
- **Symmetric skepticism:** an app's confidence score and internet plant
  folklore get identical scrutiny — both are hypotheses until characters and
  three sources say otherwise. "The app was 98% sure" is not a source.
- **Health-domain precedence:** nothing here makes food/remedy claims; any
  use question is routed to the herbalism course's frame.

## Domain non-negotiables (with placement)

- **Identification errors can kill.** Poisonous look-alikes are first-class
  content: named in ch. 1 (why the ladder exists), embodied in ch. 2's umbel
  portrait, trained in ch. 3 (the look-alike check is a mandatory workflow
  step). NOTHING crosses from "identified" to "usable" without the
  three-source rule AND the herbalism course's safety frame — stated in
  ch. 1's boundary paragraph, enforced in ch. 3, restated in ch. 5's crib
  with the explicit cross-link to herbalism ch. 7 (red flags) + ch. 8 (the
  rule's origin).
- **Never taste or forage as an identification method** — ch. 2 (senses:
  smell yes, taste never) and ch. 3 (the workflow has no taste step).
- **Collecting legality and ethics** — ch. 4: protected species, protected
  areas, private land come before technique; never collect rarities;
  leave-more-than-you-take; photograph where doubt or scarcity says don't
  pick. Restated as the flora's standing ethic in ch. 5.
- **No invented biography.** The owners' real relationship to plants stays
  within `persona-context.md` facts; hooks use honest hypotheticals
  ("imagine…") or verified moments only. Cross-links to herbalism claim only
  what that course actually teaches.

## Editorial notes (kept small)

- **Apps are genuinely good and getting better** — the course never
  app-bashes. Their honest failure modes (grasses, sedges, juveniles,
  regional twins) are exactly why they stay hypothesis generators; a reader
  who learns characters gets *more* out of an app, not less. Stated plainly
  in ch. 3.
- **A herbarium takes life** — pressing sits in real tension with
  leave-no-trace. The course's answer: keep it tiny (5 sheets, common plants
  only, one specimen where many grow) and let photography carry everything
  scarce or doubtful. Named honestly in ch. 4, not hidden.
- **Latin binomials** are taught as the one unambiguous handle (folk names
  collide across regions; a binomial never does) — but folk names are kept as
  the relationship layer, and the record carries both. Small note in ch. 3.
