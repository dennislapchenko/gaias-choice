1. **Verdict** — one or two sentences for the reader who leaves after ten
   seconds: would we buy it again, knowing what we know now?
2. **Why we bought it** — nobody wakes up wanting a review; they wake up with
   a problem at 3 a.m. What real problem in our day on the road did this have
   to solve, what were we afraid would go wrong, what were we choosing
   between?
3. **How it lived with us** — how long, in what conditions (camper, road,
   baby)? Bought or gifted (gifted must be said out loud)? What broke, what
   did we repair, what would we genuinely miss if it vanished tomorrow?
4. **What's genuinely good** — concrete beats adjectives: counted washes,
   measured grams, real prices. What's the one specific moment it earned its
   place?
5. **What we'd change** — every review names a flaw; if nothing else, the
   flaw is the price, or who it's *not* for. And the question a paid reviewer
   never answers: what would make us take this recommendation back?
6. **Who it's for** — and who should skip it, one honest line each. How long
   could we happily do without it?
