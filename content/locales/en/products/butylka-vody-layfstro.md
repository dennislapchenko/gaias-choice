1. **Verdict** — one or two sentences for the reader who leaves after ten
   seconds: would we buy the LifeStraw bottle again, knowing what we know now?
2. **Why we bought it** — nobody wakes up wanting a review; they wake up with
   a problem. Whose water were we not willing to drink — a campsite tap, a
   spring, a stranger's hose — and what were we afraid of with a baby in the
   van? What were we choosing between: bottled water, boiling, tablets,
   another filter?
3. **How it lived with us** — how long, and where did we actually fill it:
   taps, springs, streams? Bought or gifted (gifted must be said out loud)?
   How is the filter holding up and have we replaced one yet, does it leak in
   a bag, what would we genuinely miss if it vanished tomorrow?
4. **What's genuinely good** — concrete beats adjectives: what it holds, how
   hard it is to suck through, what a replacement filter costs, how many
   bought bottles it has replaced. What's the one specific fill where it
   earned its place?
5. **What we'd change** — every review names a flaw; if nothing else, the flaw
   is the price, or who it's *not* for. Plastic taste, flow rate, filter cost,
   what it does *not* filter — and the question a paid reviewer never answers:
   what would make us take this recommendation back?
6. **Who it's for** — and who should skip it, one honest line each. How long
   could we happily do without it — is it for daily use or for the day the tap
   looks wrong?
