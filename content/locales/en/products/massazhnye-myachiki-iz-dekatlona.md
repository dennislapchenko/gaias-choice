1. **Verdict** — one or two sentences for the reader who leaves after ten
   seconds: would we buy the Decathlon massage balls again, knowing what we
   know now?
2. **Why we bought it** — nobody wakes up wanting a review; they wake up with
   a problem — in this case a body. What hurts after a day of driving and
   carrying a baby, and what were we doing about it before? What were we
   choosing between: a roller, hands, stretching, nothing?
3. **How it lived with us** — how long, how often, and where do they get used:
   on the camper floor, against a wall, on the ground outside? Bought or
   gifted (gifted must be said out loud)? Have they gone soft, split, or gone
   missing under a seat, and what would we genuinely miss if they vanished
   tomorrow?
4. **What's genuinely good** — concrete beats adjectives: what they weigh, how
   little space they take, what they cost, how often we actually reach for
   them in a week. What's the one specific ache they fixed?
5. **What we'd change** — every review names a flaw; if nothing else, the flaw
   is the price, or who it's *not* for. Hardness, size, smell out of the
   packet — and the question a paid reviewer never answers: what would make us
   take this recommendation back?
6. **Who it's for** — and who should skip it, one honest line each. How long
   could we happily do without them — do hands and a doorframe do the same
   job?
