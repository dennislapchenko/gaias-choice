1. **Verdict** — one or two sentences for the reader who leaves after ten
   seconds: would we buy the baby nest again, knowing what we know now?
2. **Why we bought it** — nobody wakes up wanting a review; they wake up with
   a problem at 3 a.m. What were we worried about — a newborn on a big
   mattress, sleeping in a moving-house, nowhere safe to put her down awake?
   What were we choosing between: co-sleeping as-is, a travel cot, a basket?
3. **How it lived with us** — how long, and where did it actually get used:
   the camper bed, outside in the shade, at other people's houses? Bought or
   gifted (gifted must be said out loud)? Has it stayed clean and firm, how
   does the cover wash and dry on the road, and what would we genuinely miss
   if it vanished tomorrow?
4. **What's genuinely good** — concrete beats adjectives: what it weighs, how
   fast it moves from bed to floor to outside, what it's made of, what it
   cost. What's the one specific night or afternoon it earned its place?
5. **What we'd change** — every review names a flaw; if nothing else, the flaw
   is the price, or who it's *not* for. The months it's useful for, the
   materials, the safety caveats we'd want a parent to read first — and the
   question a paid reviewer never answers: what would make us take this
   recommendation back?
6. **Who it's for** — and who should skip it, one honest line each. How long
   could we happily do without it — how quickly did the baby outgrow it?
