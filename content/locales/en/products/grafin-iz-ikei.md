1. **Verdict** — one or two sentences for the reader who leaves after ten
   seconds: would we buy the IKEA carafe again, knowing what we know now?
2. **Why we bought it** — nobody wakes up wanting a review; they wake up with
   a problem. What was it replacing — plastic bottles, a jug that leaked, warm
   water nobody wanted to drink? What were we afraid of in a camper (glass,
   spills, breakage), and what were we choosing between?
3. **How it lived with us** — how long, and where does it actually live while
   driving? Bought or gifted (gifted must be said out loud)? Has it chipped or
   broken, does the lid still seal, how does it wash without a proper sink,
   what would we genuinely miss if it vanished tomorrow?
4. **What's genuinely good** — concrete beats adjectives: what it holds, what
   it cost, how it pours one-handed with a baby on the other arm. What's the
   one specific moment it earned its place?
5. **What we'd change** — every review names a flaw; if nothing else, the flaw
   is the price, or who it's *not* for. Glass in a moving vehicle, the lid,
   the shape — and the question a paid reviewer never answers: what would make
   us take this recommendation back?
6. **Who it's for** — and who should skip it, one honest line each. How long
   could we happily do without it — is this a want or a real fix?
